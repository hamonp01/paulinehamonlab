Research

Macrophages are among the most abundant immune cells in tumors and play a central role in shaping cancer progression and response to therapy. However, macrophages are not a single cell type: they encompass diverse populations with distinct origins, molecular programs, and functions. The Hamon Lab aims to decipher macrophage diversity in cancer and understand how distinct macrophage states influence tumor development, anti-tumor immunity, and treatment resistance.

Macrophage Diversity

How do tissue-resident and monocyte-derived macrophages contribute to cancer development?

Macrophages are shaped by both their developmental origin and the signals they receive from their environment. In liver cancers and other solid tumors, tissue-resident macrophages and monocyte-derived macrophages coexist and adopt diverse functional states, but their specific roles remain incompletely understood.

The lab investigates how macrophage populations emerge, evolve, and organize within adult and pediatric liver cancers, with the goal of identifying conserved and context-specific macrophage programs across solid tumors.

Macrophage Functions

How do macrophages shape tumor progression and anti-tumor immunity?

Beyond their diversity, macrophages can have opposing functions within tumors, either supporting anti-tumor immunity or promoting immune suppression and disease progression. We study how macrophages interact with tumor cells and other immune cells to shape the tumor ecosystem. Using genetically engineered preclinical models and functional perturbation approaches, we investigate how macrophage origin, identity, and molecular programs influence tumor growth, immune responses, and the emergence of resistant tumor states.

Macrophage Targeting

How can macrophage diversity be targeted to develop new therapeutic strategies?

Resistance to therapy and relapse post treatment remain a major challenge, including in liver cancers. We investigate how macrophage programs contribute to resistance to immunotherapy in hepatocellular carcinoma and chemotherapy in hepatoblastoma. By integrating patient cohorts, preclinical models, and ex vivo human tumor platforms, we aim to identify macrophage targets and develop strategies to reprogram the tumor microenvironment and restore treatment sensitivity.

Research Approaches
